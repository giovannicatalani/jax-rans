    A swept wing section (2.5D)¶

    One station of a swept wing, computed as an infinite swept wing: the section is the same all along the sweep line, so nothing varies along it. The grid is an ordinary 2D grid; the flow has three velocity components. It does not capture taper, the root or the tip: a wing assembled from 2.5D stations needs the stations' effective incidence from a lifting-line or vortex-lattice model of the planform.

    1. The case¶

    Add sweep_deg to the freestream of any 2D case:

    "domain": {"grid": "c_grid", "airfoil": "rae2822.dat", "...": "...", "section": "streamwise"},
"freestream": {"mach": 0.842, "alpha_deg": 2.0, "sweep_deg": 30.0, "reynolds": 6.5e6, "temperature": 460.0}
    • mach and alpha_deg are the freestream's: the Mach number of the whole velocity, and the incidence in the streamwise plane.
    • domain.section says which section the airfoil (or grid file) is: streamwise (the default: the cut parallel to the freestream, as wings are usually defined) or normal (the cut normal to the sweep line). A streamwise section is meshed normal to the sweep line: the solver scales the grid's x by cos(sweep), which keeps the thickness on a shorter chord (a 12 % streamwise section is 13.9 % thick normal to a 30 degree sweep line).
    • Lengths stay in streamwise units: reynolds is per unit length as in 2D, and reference_length and moment_centre are streamwise.
    • The far field needs the Mach number in the plane of the grid below 1, M sqrt(cos^2(alpha) cos^2(sweep) + sin^2(alpha)), so a supersonic freestream over a subsonic leading edge is accepted. Both far-field models (freestream, point_vortex) work.
    • sweep_deg: 0 runs the 2.5D path without sweep: the 2D solution, with a zero spanwise velocity.

    examples/rae2822_swept_sa is a worked case.

    2. The frames¶

      top view                                   the grid (normal section)
            S (span)
            ^     / sweep line, z                   y
            |   /                                    ^
            | /  sweep                               |  ___
            +--------> X (freestream)                | /   \______  airfoil, as in 2D
             \                                       +--------------> x
              \ x (normal chord)                    z: out of the page, along the sweep line

    In the grid's frame (x along the normal chord, y up, z along the sweep line) the freestream is

    V = M (cos(alpha) cos(sweep),  sin(alpha),  cos(alpha) sin(sweep))

    an in-plane flow at the in-plane Mach number and incidence atan(tan(alpha) / cos(sweep)), plus a uniform spanwise component. The equations are the 3D ones with every derivative along z zero; the state carries rho w as a fifth mean-flow row (U = (rho, rho u, rho v, rho w, E[, rho nu~])).

    3. What comes out¶

    • Force coefficients in the wing frame, per unit span, per q_inf (the whole freestream) times reference_length: CL normal to the freestream in the vertical plane, CD along it, CM about moment_centre in streamwise coordinates (pressure only, as in 2D), and CY, the spanwise force. Pressure acts normal to the swept generators, so it has a spanwise part, -tan(sweep) times its streamwise part; friction adds to it.
    • Surface (*_surface.dat): x in the normal section, x_streamwise (x / cos(sweep), the station's streamwise coordinate), y, cp, and viscous, cf along the chord and cf_z along the sweep line; all over the whole q_inf. cp is the pressure itself, so it compares directly with a 3D wing's cp at the station, plotted at x_streamwise.
    • Fields (*_fields.npz): U with its rho w row.

    4. Limits¶

    • Fully turbulent (SA, SA-QCR): no crossflow transition.
    • An infinite wing: no taper, root or tip effects.
    • 2D grids only; derivatives (jaxrans.sensitivity) are in alpha and mach at fixed sweep.
